Exploring environments that are hazardous or inaccessible to humans is one of the most compelling frontiers in robotics. Natural terrains filled with granular materials—like sand—present unique challenges for robotic movement and control. During my graduate studies, I worked as a Research Assistant in the Bioinspired Robotics and Design Lab at UC San Diego, where I focused on soft, everting vine robots. These flexible robots can extend their bodies to navigate complex environments, and have shown great potential for use in granular media. However, their reliance on tethers often limits their mobility and range.

To address this, I developed an untethered, continuously everting soft robot specifically designed for navigating and digging through granular materials. My work involved evaluating how the robot’s design reduces drag and enhances thrust generation while burrowing. Through a series of experiments, I validated these design improvements and successfully demonstrated the robot’s ability to move through granular terrain.

This work culminated in a research paper titled "Continuous Skin Eversion Enables an Untethered Soft Robot to Burrow in Granular Media," which was published and presented at the IEEE International Conference on Soft Robotics (RoboSoft) 2023 in Singapore. I was honored to be selected to give a lightning talk, providing a platform to share my research with a broader audience. Presenting at RoboSoft allowed me to engage with the soft robotics community and exchange ideas with leading researchers and pioneers in the field.
